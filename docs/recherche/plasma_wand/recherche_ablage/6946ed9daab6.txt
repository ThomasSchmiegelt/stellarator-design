Proxima Fusion is a European fusion energy company founded in 2023 in Munich [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich], Germany, as the first research spin-off [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_spin-off] from the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Plasma_Physics]. [ 1 ] Its stated goal is to design the first generation of fusion power plants using a quasi-isodynamic stellarator [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-isodynamic_stellarator] (QI stellarator). [ 2 ]
History
[ edit ]
Proxima was founded in April 2023 by Francesco Sciortino, Lucio Milanese, Jorrit Lion, Jonathan Schilling, and Martin Kubie, former scientists and engineers from the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Plasma_Physics], the Massachusetts Institute of Technology [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology], and X Development [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Development] (was Google-X). [ 3 ]
The company initially raised €7.5 million in pre-seed funding from Plural Platform, UVC Partners, Visionaries Club, Wilbe, High-Tech Gründerfonds [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-Tech_Gründerfonds], and others, followed by a €20 million seed round led by redalpine, with participation from Bayern Kapital, DeepTech & Climate Fonds, and the Max Planck Foundation. [ 4 ] It has since been awarded more public funding from the European Innovation Council [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Innovation_Council] (source) and the Federal Ministry of Education and Research (Germany) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Education_and_Research_(Germany)]. [ 5 ]
On June 28, 2024, Proxima announced that it would partner with the Paul Scherrer Institute [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Scherrer_Institute] to develop high-temperature superconducting [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-temperature_superconductivity] magnets for its stellarators. [ 6 ]
In February 2025, Proxima presented a concept for a commercial fusion power plant named Stellaris. [ 7 ] That concept has been compared in impact to the US ARC fusion reactor [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARC_fusion_reactor] tokamak [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak] concept published in 2014. [ 8 ] A smaller prototype stellarator aiming at net fusion energy is planned for 2031. [ 9 ]
In June 2025, Proxima Fusion announced that they received €130 million in funding in a Series A round [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_A_round]. [ 10 ] [ 11 ] In September 2025, they received an added €15M extension. [ 12 ]
An investment round of July 2026 included Google parent company Alphabet Inc. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_Inc.] [ 13 ]
Technology
[ edit ]
Since 2015 the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics has operated the quasi-isodynamic stellarator Wendelstein 7-X [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendelstein_7-X]. It follows in a series of Max Planck stellarators built and operated since 1960. The quasi-isodynamic feature was developed theoretically and in practical implementation in the 1980s by Allen Boozer [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Boozer] and Jürgen Nührenberg [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Nührenberg], for which they were awarded the 2010 Hannes Alfvén Prize [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannes_Alfvén_Prize] by the European Physical Society [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society]. [ 14 ] [ 15 ]
Max Planck developed a series of reactor design studies based on Wendelstein 7-X since the 1990s. Proxima Fusion's Stellaris design is substantially similar but with a higher magnetic field provided by recent developments in high temperature superconductors, and correspondingly a somewhat smaller size. In both Wendelstein 7-X and Stellaris, toroidal plasma current is optimized to cancel to near-zero, which is believed to contribute to stability and steady state operation. [ 16 ] [ 17 ]
