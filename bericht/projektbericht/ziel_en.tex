\section{A coil set at the target: DPrcxq66 meets all limits in the standard mesh (not in the finer mesh)}\label{sec:ziel}

\subsection*{Final state after validation (authoritative)}
Stress is now the p99 of the hottest coil (without boundary zone); the guideline value of 500\,MPa is an assumption, not a fixed limit. Set: DPrcxq66, walls $\times1.6$, deliberately thinned plasma side of the wall (clearance at least 1.5\,m), connectors placed by gaps, wall thickness adapted per coil.

\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
Variant & hottest coil & strain & clearance \\ \hline
Standard mesh & 446\,MPa & 0.25\,\% & 1.51\,m \\
Mesh $\times1.5$ & 432\,MPa & 0.27\,\% & 1.51\,m \\
Mesh $\times2$ & 531\,MPa & 0.29\,\% & 1.50\,m \\
Cool-down (case B, assumed) & 447\,MPa & 0.31\,\% & 1.51\,m \\ \hline

\smallskip
\noindent The submodel of the hottest coil deviates by at most 6\,\% from the global model (446, 474, 461\,MPa). The walls were over-dimensioned: a wall target of 500\,MPa
instead of 450\,MPa needs a factor 1.17 instead of 1.3 at the same value of the hottest coil. The set counts provisionally as launch base \emph{by guideline values}; mesh $\times2$ and cool-down case B are just above.
The earlier interim states below (average over all coils, fixed connector plans) are superseded: the failure in the finer mesh was a procedural error of the fixed connector plan, the clearance of 1.56\,m a coincidence of the untangling step.


\textbf{Reading aid.} ``At the target'' here means: the global model (all coils, averaged over the coils) keeps the assumed limits.
The limits are assumptions, not measured values: steel equivalent stress (p99, 99\,\% of the material lies below) at most 500\,MPa,
winding-pack strain at most 0.3\,\%, neutron fluence at most $3\cdot10^{18}$\,n/cm$^2$, shielding at least 1.0\,m.
The clearance is the free distance between the plasma edge and the inner side of the coil casing.

The user wanted us to ``bring one to the target, also to understand'', and then use it as a launch base.

\subsection*{Path}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Place connectors by gaps: the longest stretch without connector from 17.4\,m to 4\,m, deflection from 241 to 42\,mm (DjLe3eL6).
\item Room for shielding: the set with the best plasma has too little clearance (0.47\,m). Enlarging the plant (DYU $\times1.45$) creates room, but the stress
  does not stay equal (417 to 627\,MPa). A set with more distance (DPrcxq66) was the better way.
\item Thicker walls for this set.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent DPrcxq66 has two field periods, QI quality 0.00079 (about eight times better than DP9 at 0.0068), force 792\,MN per coil.

\subsection*{Expectation beforehand and comparison}
Expected were 460 to 520\,MPa ($\times1.3$) and 400 to 450\,MPa ($\times1.6$); found 453 and 384\,MPa, slightly better. The strain ranges were met,
only DGHe48vB was slightly above the expected 0.30 to 0.33\,\% with 0.34\,\%.

\subsection*{Limits of the statement}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item The limits are assumptions. The submodel of the hottest coil gives 602 to 605\,MPa, above 500\,MPa. Which measure should apply is open.
\item The clearance of 1.32\,m lies just above the documented HELIAS value of 1.3\,m; tile and tolerance values are missing.
\item \textbf{Standard mesh only.} In the finer mesh (stations $\times1.5$, fixed connector plan) DPrcxq66 ($\times1.6$) fails: steel p99 580\,MPa, strain 0.35\,\%, clearance 1.19\,m (instead of 1.56\,m), fluence $4.3\cdot10^{18}$. Expected were $+10$ to $+30$\,\%, found $+51$\,\%. Cool-down (three assumed cases) holds except case B (strain 0.33\,\%). The set is therefore \emph{not} a secured launch base.
\item QI measures the field shape; confinement and stability are not calculated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Two corrections and a test}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item The force scan of the coil sets divides by the field $B_0$ from the dataset metrics; the actual coil field differs from set to set. The ranking by scan force is therefore wrong. The earlier statement that sets with more distance have three to ten times the force was false: DPrcxq66 has only 1.4 times that of DjLe3eL6 (792 versus 564\,MN per coil).
\item \emph{Two coil rings} (idea of the user; inner ring at 0.85\,$a$, outer at 1.3\,$a$): the field error drops on average to two fifths, the largest force does not drop ($+7.5$\,\% to $-3.8$\,\%). The single ring at 1.3\,$a$ needs $+53$\,\% force compared with 0.85\,$a$. None of the optimisations converged; the comparison with the Proxima set is not equivalent.
\end{itemize}
